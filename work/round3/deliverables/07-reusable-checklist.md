# SaaS Subscription Contract: 12-Point Review Checklist

Built from the Skyline review and generalized for any SaaS, data-platform, or device-telemetry subscription. Run it before signature; a single "no" on a red-line item should stop the deal, not just lower the price.

## A. Scope and price

1. **Is the deliverable in writing?** Features, seat counts, data retention, and support response times must live in an exhibit, not on a vendor web page that can change tomorrow.
2. **Does payment track delivery?** At minimum split signature and production acceptance. Annual prepayment with no acceptance gate is a credit decision, not a purchase.
3. **Is the price fixed for the term?** A unilateral right to raise fees mid-term makes the consideration illusory. Cap renewal increases (5% is a common landing zone).
4. **Is early termination priced rationally?** Acceleration of all remaining fees is a penalty; expect it to be reduced and negotiate a pro rata refund instead.

## B. Performance and remedy

5. **Is the service level backed by money?** A credit capped at 5% of one month, declared the sole remedy, invites a failure-of-essential-purpose argument under UCC 2-719(2). Push for credits that scale and a termination right at the bottom.
6. **Whose outages are excluded?** If the vendor's hosting provider or subcontractors are carved out, the vendor has sold you its infrastructure risk.
7. **Is there an express warranty?** "AS IS" plus no affirmative promise leaves nothing to enforce. Add material conformance to the exhibit and workmanlike performance.

## C. Data (the red-line zone)

8. **What does the data license actually permit?** Scan for "perpetual," "irrevocable," "worldwide," "train," "improve models," "derived data," "business purposes." Each one converts your data into their asset.
9. **Is there a service-provider agreement?** Where CCPA applies, Cal. Civ. Code section 1798.100(d) requires five specific terms: limited and specified purposes, compliance obligation, audit rights, notice of inability to comply, and a right to stop and remediate.
10. **What happens after a breach?** Fixed notice window (72 hours is workable), cooperation, and cost-bearing for notification and credit monitoring. Remember section 1798.150 statutory damages of $100-$750 per consumer per incident sit outside any fee cap.

## D. Risk allocation and exit

11. **Does the cap cover the right things?** Verify the cap base (fees paid vs. fees payable), and carve out gross negligence, willful misconduct, security breach, and indemnity. A clause barring any court from adjusting the cap does not bind the court.
12. **Can you actually leave?** Check assignment to competitors, suspension without notice, unilateral amendment by posting, and whether termination for cause survives a disputed cure.

## How to use this list

- Red-line items: 8, 9, 10, 12. These are governance failures, not price terms.
- Trade items: 3, 4, 11. Concede one to hold the red-line items.
- Always verify the authority, not the folklore. Two citations that circulate constantly and should be checked every time: the CCPA service-provider clause is section 1798.100(d), and 16 CFR Part 425 does not currently impose a general click-to-cancel duty.
