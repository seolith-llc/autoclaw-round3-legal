# step006 - Six-jurisdiction overlay for the Northwind footprint

## Request

```text
Do we need one for each state?

Virginia, DC, MD, Delaware plus remote staff in California, Texas
```

## Answer given

No. A choice-of-law clause selects one body of contract law, so formation, remedies, warranty,
and forum analysis happen once. What varies state to state is the regulatory overlay, because
those statutes attach by residence of the person, not by what the contract says.

## What was built

`work/round3/deliverables/08-state-overlay.xlsx`, three sheets:

1. **Footprint** - six jurisdictions with regime, citation, applicability test, whether workforce
   data is covered, and status (Driver / Conditional / No).
2. **DPA requirements** - eight processor-contract requirements with verified citations.
3. **Clause map** - which redline section carries each requirement, and the remaining gap.

Plus a new Section 6 in the review memo, a closing section in the checklist, a row in the entry
pack and matter brief, and five new entries in the citation log.

## The finding

Five of the six comprehensive statutes exclude commercial and employment data from "consumer":
Virginia (59.1-575), Maryland (14-4601(H)), Delaware (12D-102(8)), Texas (541.001(7)).
California does not - CCPA covers employees and job applicants.

The platform's personal data is badge numbers, operator names, and shift records. So California
is the only jurisdiction in this footprint where that data is protected data, and the DPA is
therefore California-shaped. Virginia supplies the processor-duty template (59.1-579(B)):
confidentiality, delete/return, demonstrate compliance, allow assessments, flow down to
subcontractors.

Applicability caveats recorded rather than smoothed over:
- Delaware's threshold is 35,000 consumers, or 10,000 plus more than 20% of revenue from selling
  personal data. A 400-person manufacturer's own workforce does not reach it.
- Texas exempts small businesses under the SBA definition (541.002(a)), and loses the exemption
  if sensitive data is sold.
- Maryland's threshold figure was truncated in the source read, so the overlay describes it
  qualitatively instead of quoting a number.
- DC has no comprehensive privacy statute; obligations come from the contract, the CPPA, and
  breach-notification law.
